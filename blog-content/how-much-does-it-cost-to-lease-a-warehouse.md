Warehouse lease costs in the United States range from $7 to $22 per square foot annually in 2026, depending on your region, building class, and lease structure. The national average asking rent sits at $9.12 per square foot (Cushman & Wakefield Q2 2025 Industrial MarketBeat). In South Florida — one of the tightest industrial markets in the country — rates run $10–18 per square foot NNN. Understanding what you will actually pay requires breaking down base rent, operating expenses, and one-time move-in costs before you sign anything.

## What You Actually Pay: Base Rent vs. Total Occupancy Cost

The number a landlord quotes you — say, $12.00 per square foot — is almost never the number that hits your bank account each month. Warehouse leases split costs into two layers: base rent and operating expenses.

**Base rent** is the negotiated rate per square foot, multiplied by your leased square footage, divided by 12 for a monthly figure. A 5,000 SF space at $12.00/SF annually costs $5,000 per month in base rent.

**Operating expenses** (also called NNN charges on a triple net lease) are passed through to the tenant on top of base rent. According to [WareSpace's 2026 warehouse cost guide](https://warespace.com/resources/cost-to-rent-a-warehouse), NNN charges typically add $1.50–3.00 per square foot annually, covering property taxes, building insurance, common area maintenance (CAM), landscaping and parking lot upkeep, and property management fees.

On a 5,000 SF space with $2.00/SF in NNN charges, that adds $833 per month. According to [WareSpace's cost breakdown](https://warespace.com/resources/cost-to-rent-a-warehouse), the real monthly cost on a traditional NNN lease runs 25–40% above the quoted rate once operating expenses and utilities are included. A 1,500 SF space with a $1,300/month quoted base rent can easily land at $1,800/month all-in once NNN items are added.

**One-time and upfront costs** add another layer to budget for: security deposit (typically 1–3 months of base rent), first and last month's rent, tenant improvement work beyond any landlord allowance, and utility connection fees. (2026 Rates)](https://palmbeachwarehouses.com/blog/warehouse-space-cost-palm-beach-county/).

## How Lease Type Changes Your Total Bill

Lease structure is the single most important variable most tenants overlook when comparing properties. The same $12.00/SF base rent can cost you very different amounts depending on whether the lease is NNN, modified gross, or full-service gross.

| Lease Type | What Tenant Pays | What Landlord Pays | Typical Market |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triple Net (NNN) | Base rent + taxes + insurance + CAM | Structure and roof | Most industrial/warehouse |
| Modified Gross | Base rent + utilities (sometimes) | Taxes, insurance, CAM | Smaller flex/office-warehouse |
| Full-Service Gross | Base rent only | All operating expenses | Rare in industrial; common in office |
| Absolute NNN | Base rent + all expenses including roof/structure | Nothing | Single-tenant net-leased investments |

NNN is the overwhelming standard for warehouse and industrial space in South Florida and nationally. According to [WareCRE's warehouse lease type guide](https://warecre.com/cre-insights/industrial-101/warehouse-lease-types-explained/), triple net is the dominant lease structure in industrial real estate, and every NNN quote should have $2–4/SF added to estimate total occupancy cost. When you see a quoted rate on LoopNet or a broker's flyer, assume NNN unless stated otherwise.

For a deeper comparison of NNN vs. gross lease structures and what each costs over a typical 3–5 year lease term, read [NNN vs. Gross Lease: A Warehouse Tenant's Guide for Palm Beach County](https://palmbeachwarehouses.com/blog/nnn-vs-gross-lease-warehouse-palm-beach-county/).

## What Drives Warehouse Lease Rates

Six factors determine where your space lands within the $7–22/SF range.

**Location and logistics access.** Proximity to major highways, ports, and population centers can add 15–25% to lease rates. According to [Red Stag Fulfillment's 2025 warehousing rate analysis](https://redstagfulfillment.com/warehousing-rates-per-square-foot-in-us/) (citing Cushman & Wakefield Q2 2025 data), coastal markets consistently command 50–100% premiums over secondary inland markets for comparable product.

**Building size.** Smaller spaces cost more per square foot. According to [Red Stag Fulfillment's 2025 analysis](https://redstagfulfillment.com/warehousing-rates-per-square-foot-in-us/) (citing Cushman & Wakefield Q2 2025 data), warehouses under 100,000 SF average $9.51/SF nationally versus $7.26/SF for spaces over 100,000 SF — a 31% premium. For small businesses leasing 2,000–20,000 SF, plan for rates above the headline national average.

**Clear height.** Ceiling height directly affects usable cubic volume and what you can do with the space. Standard warehouse product runs 18–24 feet clear; Class A distribution facilities reach 32–40 feet. According to [Rise Commercial District's warehouse renting guide](https://risecommercialdistrict.com/how-to-rent-a-warehouse/), ceiling height is one of the first requirements to nail down before touring — a space that looks right on paper fails immediately if your racking or equipment needs more vertical clearance than the building allows. Higher clear heights command 10–20% rent premiums in most markets.

**Building class.** Class A (built after 2010, modern dock doors, ESFR sprinklers, LED lighting, heavy three-phase power) commands top-of-market rents. Class B offers most functional features at a 10–25% discount. Class C (older construction, lower ceilings, limited dock access) runs cheapest but carries higher operating costs. According to [Rise Commercial District](https://risecommercialdistrict.com/how-to-rent-a-warehouse/), right-sizing both the space class and footprint prevents the two most common first-lease mistakes: paying for unused Class A specs you do not need, or getting locked into Class C space you quickly outgrow.

**Loading configuration.** Dock-high doors (for semi-trucks) add significant cost versus drive-in/grade-level access. A dock-high space in a tight market can run $1–3/SF more than comparable drive-in product, reflecting the operational value for distribution-oriented tenants.

**Market vacancy.** According to [Red Stag Fulfillment's 2025 analysis](https://redstagfulfillment.com/warehousing-rates-per-square-foot-in-us/) (citing Cushman & Wakefield Q2 2025 data), national industrial vacancy climbed to 7.1% — the highest in over a decade — as speculative development outpaced absorption. But small-bay space under 50,000 SF remains structurally undersupplied, with vacancy well below the headline figure in most Sun Belt markets. In South Florida, landlords of small-bay product face little pressure to negotiate on rate even as large-format industrial softens.

## Regional Warehouse Lease Rates in 2026

Geography is the biggest variable in the cost equation. The same 10,000 SF warehouse costs roughly twice as much in Los Angeles as it does in Memphis.

| Region | Annual Rate Range ($/SF) | Vacancy Rate | Market Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| West Coast (LA, Bay Area, Seattle) | $13–22/SF | 4.5–6.5% | Flat to -3% YoY |
| Northeast (NJ, Boston, Baltimore) | $8–11/SF | 7–9% | Modest softening |
| Sun Belt (Miami, Dallas, Nashville) | $8.50–12+/SF | 3–6% | +2–4% YoY growth |
| Mid-America (Chicago, Indianapolis, Memphis) | $6–8/SF | 8–11% | Stable, oversupplied |
| Inland Empire (CA) | $12–16/SF | 5–5.5% | -2% to +2% YoY |

Source: Cushman & Wakefield and CBRE Q4 2025 industrial market data, as cited in Red Stag Fulfillment's 2025 warehousing rate analysis.

Sun Belt markets stand out because low vacancy sustains rent growth even as other regions soften. According to [WareCRE's Miami Warehouse Market Report 2025](https://warecre.com/cre-insights/market-property-insights/miami-warehouse-market-report-2025/), Miami warehouse lease rates average $14.50 per square foot NNN, with Class A product in Doral commanding $14–18/SF. Broward County, according to [WareSpace's Fort Lauderdale market report Q1 2026](https://warespace.com/resources/market-reports/fort-lauderdale), averages $17.34–17.67/SF NNN — among the highest in Florida.

## What Warehouse Leasing Costs in South Florida

South Florida operates in a different cost tier than the national average. The region's combination of port access (Port of Miami, Port Everglades, Port of Palm Beach), population density, and limited industrial land supply creates structural scarcity that keeps rents elevated year over year.

**Miami-Dade County:** $14–18/SF NNN for Class A, $10–14/SF for Class B (WareCRE, 2025).

**Broward County (Fort Lauderdale area):** $17.34–17.67/SF NNN average asking rent Q1 2026 (WareSpace, 2026).

**Palm Beach County:** Rates generally run below Miami-Dade and Broward, reflecting the county's northern position and more available industrial land. Small-bay product in Riviera Beach and Lake Worth — closest to Broward — tracks closer to Broward pricing. Northern submarkets (West Palm Beach, Jupiter, Palm Beach Gardens) tend to run $1–3/SF below the Miami corridor, offering a cost-effective option for businesses that do not need proximity to Miami port logistics. According to [WareCRE's South Florida industrial analysis](https://warecre.com/cre-insights/market-property-insights/miami-warehouse-market-report-2025/), spillover demand from Miami-Dade and Broward has steadily pushed Palm Beach County rents higher as tenants priced out of southern markets look north for value.

For a business leasing 3,000–15,000 SF in Palm Beach County, a realistic all-in monthly cost includes base rent of $10–16/SF NNN annually ($2,500–$20,000/month depending on size), NNN charges of $1.50–$3.00/SF annually, and utilities that vary by use. Total occupancy cost typically runs $12–21/SF annually.

## How to Build a Realistic Lease Budget

Before you start touring spaces, work through this five-step budget exercise.

**Step 1 — Calculate your square footage need.** An undersized space forces an early relocation; an oversized space wastes rent from day one. Our guide on [How Much Warehouse Space Do You Need?](https://palmbeachwarehouses.com/blog/how-much-warehouse-space-do-you-need/) walks through this calculation for Palm Beach County operators.

**Step 2 — Estimate total occupancy cost, not just base rent.** Take the quoted NNN rate, add $2/SF for operating expenses as a baseline, and add $0.50–$1.50/SF for utilities. That is your per-square-foot occupancy cost. According to [WareSpace's cost guide](https://warespace.com/resources/cost-to-rent-a-warehouse), tenants who skip this step routinely underestimate their first-year costs by 25–40%.

**Step 3 — Factor in upfront cash.** Security deposit (1–3 months), first month's rent, and any tenant improvement work above the landlord allowance. A 5,000 SF space at $12/SF with a 2-month deposit and $3/SF of tenant work above the allowance requires roughly $25,000–$30,000 upfront before operations begin.

**Step 4 — Model rent escalations.** Standard industrial leases include annual rent bumps of 2–4%, according to [WareCRE's lease type analysis](https://warecre.com/cre-insights/industrial-101/warehouse-lease-types-explained/). On a 5-year lease at $12/SF with 3% annual increases, your year-5 rate is $13.91/SF — a 16% increase over your starting figure. Budget for this from day one.

**Step 5 — Know where to negotiate.** In a market where small-bay vacancy sits below 5%, landlords hold more pricing power on base rent than they did in 2023–2024. According to [Rise Commercial District's leasing guide](https://risecommercialdistrict.com/how-to-rent-a-warehouse/), the search and negotiation phase for a small business typically takes 30–60 days including tours, proposal reviews, and lease back-and-forth. The gains today are on tenant improvements (TI), free-rent periods, and operating expense caps rather than base rent reduction. A local sales associate who works Palm Beach County submarkets daily knows which landlords are open to concessions and which hold firm.

## FAQ

**How much is a typical warehouse lease per month?**
A 5,000 SF warehouse in Palm Beach County running $12/SF NNN costs $5,000/month in base rent plus roughly $700–$1,000/month in NNN charges — total monthly cost of $5,700–$6,000. Larger spaces (10,000–20,000 SF) at similar rates run $10,000–$20,000+ per month all-in.

**Is it cheaper to buy or lease warehouse space?**
For most businesses under 20,000 SF, leasing is less capital-intensive in the near term because purchase prices for South Florida industrial have climbed significantly in the post-pandemic cycle. Leasing preserves working capital and maintains flexibility to scale or relocate. Buying makes sense when you need specialized infrastructure, plan to stay 10+ years, or can acquire at a below-market basis.

**What is a NNN lease and how does it affect my costs?**
A triple net (NNN) lease requires you to pay property taxes, building insurance, and common area maintenance on top of base rent. According to [WareCRE's lease type guide](https://warecre.com/cre-insights/industrial-101/warehouse-lease-types-explained/), NNN charges add $2–4/SF annually in most markets on top of quoted base rent. The advantage for tenants is full transparency: you see exactly where operating costs go each year, and you benefit directly if expenses come in below estimate.

**How long are warehouse leases typically?**
Small-bay warehouse leases in South Florida commonly run 3–5 years for spaces under 10,000 SF. Spaces over 20,000 SF often require 5–7 year terms. Shorter terms (1–2 years) are available but come at a rent premium because landlords price in rollover risk.

**What is a tenant improvement allowance?**
A TI allowance is a credit the landlord provides toward improvements in the space — lighting upgrades, new dock levelers, office build-out, floor coatings. In South Florida's tight market, TI allowances for small-bay spaces typically range from $5–15/SF. Any work beyond that allowance comes out of the tenant's pocket.
